Soldering 101: Tips Every Beginner Should Know

Published: Aug 2026

Introduction

Reliable soldering is the difference between a prototype that works flawlessly and one that fails intermittently on the bench. Whether you're assembling a breakout board or building a custom PCB for your FYP, mastering soldering fundamentals will save you hours of frustrating debugging.

Essential Budget Tools

You don't need expensive equipment to get started. Here's the minimum kit every beginner should have:

  • 60W Adjustable Temperature Soldering Iron — PKR 1,200 - 2,000 from local electronics markets. Adjustable temperature is key for working with different solder types and component sensitivities.
  • Rosin-core 60/40 solder wire — The standard ratio for electronics work. Rosin core eliminates the need for separate flux in most cases.
  • Soldering paste/flux — For rework and stubborn joints where additional flux helps solder flow properly.
  • Brass wire tip cleaner — Superior to wet sponges. Keeps the tip hot and clean without thermal shock.
  • Heat shrink sleeves — For insulating exposed connections and professional-looking wire joints.

Step-by-Step Soldering Method

Step 1: Tinning the Tip

Apply fresh solder to the clean tip before every joint. This thin layer of solder (called tinning) dramatically improves heat transfer and prevents oxidation.

Step 2: Heating the Joint

Place the iron tip so it touches both the component leg and the PCB pad simultaneously. Hold for 2-3 seconds to bring both surfaces to soldering temperature.

Step 3: Feeding the Solder

Feed the solder wire directly into the joint (not onto the iron). The molten solder will flow evenly across the heated pad and component lead.

Step 4: Removing in Order

Remove the solder wire first, then lift the iron away. Allow the joint to cool naturally — don't blow on it or move the component during cooling.

Identifying Joints

Good Joint

Shiny, concave cone shape resembling a small "volcano." The solder fillet smoothly transitions from pad to component lead.

Cold Joint

Dull, grainy, non-conductive blob. Usually caused by insufficient heat or moving the joint during cooling. Must be reworked.

Solder Bridge

Accidental short circuit across adjacent pads. Use solder wick or a desoldering pump to remove excess and separate the pads.

Safety First

  • Fume ventilation: Always work in a well-ventilated area or use a fume extractor. Solder fumes contain rosin particulates that irritate lungs over time.
  • Eye protection: Solder splashes and clipped leads can fly unexpectedly. Safety glasses are non-negotiable.
  • Avoiding lead exposure: If using leaded solder (60/40), wash hands thoroughly after every session. Never eat or drink at your soldering station. Consider lead-free solder for sensitive projects.

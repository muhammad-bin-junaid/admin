ESP32 vs Arduino Nano: Which Should You Buy?

July 2026

Introduction

Two boards dominate the project kits of Pakistani students: the Arduino Nano and the ESP32. Both are available locally under PKR 1,500, both have massive online communities, and both can power impressive projects. But they solve fundamentally different problems.

This guide breaks down exactly where each board shines, where it falls short, and how to decide which one fits your next project — without wasting money on specs you don't need.

Spec Comparison

Spec Arduino Nano ESP32
Processor ATmega328P — 16 MHz, 8-bit Dual-Core Xtensa — 240 MHz, 32-bit
Connectivity None Wi-Fi 802.11 b/g/n + Bluetooth 4.2/BLE
SRAM 2 KB 520 KB
Flash 32 KB 4 MB
Logic Level 5V 3.3V
GPIO Pins 22 34
ADC Channels 8 (10-bit) 18 (12-bit)
Local Price ~PKR 500–750 ~PKR 1,100–1,500

On paper, the ESP32 wins almost every category. But specs don't tell the whole story. The right board depends entirely on what you're building.

When to Pick Arduino Nano

The Nano is the better choice when your project doesn't need wireless connectivity and you're working with 5V components:

  • Simple offline robotics: Line followers, obstacle avoiders, and basic motor control. The Nano's 5V logic plays nicely with most motor drivers and sensor modules without needing level shifters.
  • 5V sensor interface: Many common sensors (HC-SR04, PIR modules, basic IR sensors) operate at 5V. The Nano reads them directly. With the ESP32, you'd need a voltage divider or logic level shifter for every 5V signal.
  • Simple motor control: Driving servos, DC motors via L298N, or stepper motors with A4988 drivers — the Nano handles these cleanly without Wi-Fi overhead eating into your processing budget.
  • Lower power draw: In deep sleep, the Nano draws significantly less current than the ESP32 with Wi-Fi enabled. For battery-powered projects that only wake occasionally, the Nano can last weeks longer.
  • Learning the basics: If this is your first microcontroller, the Nano's simplicity is a feature. No Wi-Fi stacks, no Bluetooth pairing, no dual-core scheduling — just straightforward digital I/O and analog reads.

When to Pick ESP32

The ESP32 is the clear winner when your project needs wireless communication, significant processing power, or more memory:

  • IoT web servers: Host a complete web dashboard on the ESP32 that displays sensor data, controls relays, and logs history — all without any external server. The Nano physically cannot do this.
  • Wireless data logging: Read sensors and transmit readings to a cloud service (ThingSpeak, Blynk, Firebase) over Wi-Fi. Perfect for environmental monitoring, agricultural projects, or campus weather stations.
  • Bluetooth app communication: Build a phone-controlled robot, a wireless sensor display, or a BLE beacon. The ESP32's Bluetooth 4.2/BLE support opens up an entire category of projects the Nano simply can't touch.
  • Camera and AI edge tasks: The ESP32-CAM variant can serve live video streams, run basic image recognition, and handle QR code scanning. This is edge computing on a PKR 1,200 board.
  • Complex projects with large codebases: 4MB of flash and 520KB of SRAM means you can run FreeRTOS tasks, store web assets, handle OTA updates, and still have room for your application logic.

The Verdict

Here's the honest answer: you need both.

The Arduino Nano is your prototyping workhorse. It's cheap enough to leave embedded in a finished project, simple enough to teach beginners, and reliable enough for offline builds. Keep one in your kit for motor projects, sensor reading, and quick experiments.

The ESP32 is your connected powerhouse. When your project needs to talk to the internet, communicate with a phone, or process data beyond simple if/else logic, the ESP32 is the only reasonable choice in this price range. At ~PKR 1,200, you're getting Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a dual-core 240 MHz processor.

Every Pakistani tech student should have at least one of each in their project kit. The Nano handles the simple stuff cheaply. The ESP32 handles the complex stuff wirelessly. Together, they cover 95% of the projects you'll encounter in your coursework, FYP, and personal builds.

Don't pick one. Buy both. Your future self building that FYP will thank you.
